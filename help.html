Quickstart

  1. Load a song. Open the converter and drop a Standard MIDI file (.mid) onto step 1.
  2. Check the parts. The converter has already put the two busiest parts on Synth 1 and Synth 2, the next two on MIDI 1 and MIDI 2, and the drum part on Drums. Press ▶ next to a part to hear it, and click the S1, S2, M1, M2 or Drums buttons to change where it goes.
  3. Pick the sounds. Each synth gets the TracksGM patch for its General MIDI instrument; change it if you like, and pick a drum kit.
  4. Preview. Press Play preview in step 4. Click a coloured dot to mute a part, or a box to mute some bars. If the summary says the song needs several projects, see getting a song into fewer projects.
  5. Download. Press Download pack in step 5.
  6. Play it. In Novation Components, open the pack and send it to a spare pack slot on your Circuit Tracks (it replaces whatever is in that slot). Load the pack on the Tracks and open project 1. The song's sections are its scenes, in order; if there is more than one project, carry on with project 2, 3 and so on.

What you need

  • A Novation Circuit Tracks, and Novation Components (web or desktop) to send packs to it. Packs are kept on the Tracks' microSD card.
  • A Standard MIDI file, type 0 or type 1. General MIDI files work best, because the patches and drum mapping follow General MIDI.
  • A current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). Everything happens in the browser: your file is not uploaded anywhere.

The five steps in detail

1. Load a MIDI file

Drop a file onto the box or click it to choose one. The line under the box shows the file's tempo, time signature, length and number of parts. Each MIDI track and channel becomes a separate part. Where several parts share a name (common in type 0 files), they are named by instrument and channel instead, for example Saw Lead (ch 4).

2. Choose the parts

Each row is one part of the song, with its General MIDI instrument, channel, note count and a picture of where it plays. The buttons on the right send it to a Circuit Tracks track:

ButtonTrackWhat it does
S1, S2Synth 1, Synth 2Play on the Tracks' own synths, with a TracksGM patch.
M1, M2MIDI 1, MIDI 2Send notes out over MIDI to play other gear.
DrumsDrum 1–4Spread a General MIDI drum part (channel 10) across the four drum tracks. Only drum parts can use it.

Send several parts to the same track to combine them into one: for example a brass line and a pad on Synth 2. Click a lit button again to take the part off that track. The ▶ button plays the part on its own, as it is in the file.

3. Pick the sounds

  • Synth 1 / Synth 2 patch. All 128 TracksGM patches are listed in General MIDI order, so the default is the patch matching the first part's instrument. Choosing one yourself stops it changing when you move parts around.
  • Drum kit. Std, Room, Power, Elec, 808, Jazz, Brush or Orch. Each kit is eight samples in the pack, and the drum tracks use that kit.
  • Octave. Each melodic track can be moved up or down one or two octaves.
  • MIDI 1 and MIDI 2 send on the MIDI channels set on the Tracks itself (in its Setup view), not on the file's channels.

4. Check the arrangement

The summary shows how many projects the song needs, how it is cut into scenes, how many bars each pattern holds, the busiest track's pattern count (8 is the most a track can hold in one project) and the project tempo.

The grid shows the song left to right, as it will play. Columns are patterns; the top rows mark where each project (P1, P2…, with its colour) and each scene starts, with the bar number. Each Circuit Tracks track has a row whose numbers are the pattern it plays; under it are the MIDI parts feeding it.

  • Mute a whole row: click its coloured dot. A hollow ring means muted. On Synth 1, Synth 2, MIDI 1 or MIDI 2 the dot mutes every part on that track; on Kick, Snare/Tom, Hi-hats or Perc it mutes just that drum sound.
  • Mute some bars: click a box. Hatched means muted; half-hatched means some of its bars are muted. Click again to unmute.
  • S solos a track in the preview only.

Mutes change the pack as well as the preview, and muting busy parts can bring the number of projects down. Mutes are kept by bar, so they stay put when you change the options below.

Play preview plays the song as the Tracks will: notes on the step grid, at most six starting on a step, one drum sound per step. Synth parts use simple stand-in sounds (not the TracksGM patches); drums use the kit's real samples. Changing a mute or an option while it plays carries on from the same place.

5. Build the pack

  • Project name is given to every project, numbered if there are several (Song 1, Song 2…).
  • Pack name and pack colour are how the pack appears in Components.
  • Project colours: Rainbow colours the projects Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Cyan, Blue, Purple, then repeats; graduated steps through 12 shades from red to purple; or pick one colour for all.

The pack holds the whole TracksGM Universal Circuit Tracks Pack (128 patches, 64 drum samples) plus your projects in project slots 1 onwards.

Getting a song into fewer projects

Each track can hold 8 patterns in a project, and a project has 16 scenes. A song needs more projects when its parts keep changing. Try these in order, watching the projects count and the notes under the grid:

  1. Mute what you don't need. A busy part that changes every few bars (a lead line, fills) costs the most patterns.
  2. Quantise 1/16 for MIDI that was played in rather than programmed.
  3. Repeats: ≥90% or ≥75% similar. Reuses near-identical patterns; usually only a few percent of notes change.
  4. Pattern length: 1/4 steps (8-bar patterns). Halves the patterns needed, but 16th notes no longer land exactly (the moved-notes count shows how many).
  5. Tempo ÷2 or ÷4. Longer patterns still, with coarser timing and slower tempo-synced effects.

Example: One More Time (150 bars) needs 4 projects with the defaults, and 2 with Quantise 1/16 and ≥75% similar, with no timing loss and about 3% of notes changed.

On the Circuit Tracks

  • Sending the pack. In Components, open the downloaded .circuittrackspack and send it to a spare pack slot; it replaces that slot's projects, patches and samples. Then load that pack on the Tracks (see Novation's Circuit Tracks user guide for the Packs view).
  • Projects. The song starts in project 1. In each project, the scenes hold the song's sections in order and are saved as a scene chain, so the project plays through its part of the song. When the song needs more than one project, move on to project 2, 3 and so on (the Tracks can queue the next project while one plays).
  • Sounds. Synth 1 and Synth 2 load the patches you chose (each project carries its own copy, so you can tweak them per project). Drum tracks use the chosen kit; Snare/Tom, Hi-hats and Perc switch between the kit's sounds step by step.
  • Editing. Everything is ordinary Circuit Tracks patterns and scenes, so you can edit, re-arrange and save as usual. Projects use the Chromatic scale so the notes play as written.

How drums are mapped

General MIDI drum notes are spread over the four drum tracks, using the chosen kit's samples. Each track's default sample is its most-used sound; other sounds are chosen per step.

Limits and alpha status

  • The project file format is reverse-engineered, not documented by Novation. Keep a backup of your packs.
  • Confirmed on hardware so far: packs load, synth and drum parts play, and long held notes sustain.
  • Still to confirm: the octave of notes sent on MIDI 1 and MIDI 2, and scenes playing through in order with each lasting its full length.
  • Not converted: tempo changes, time-signature changes, pitch bend, controllers (volume, pan, sustain pedal) and program changes within a part.
  • The preview's synth sounds are rough stand-ins; listen on the Tracks for the real patches.

Please report problems, with the MIDI file if you can, in the project's GitHub Issues.

Troubleshooting

The page looks out of date, or a fix hasn't appeared.
Your browser kept an old copy. Reload with Cmd + Shift + R (Mac) or Ctrl + Shift + R (Windows).
"Could not load the TracksGM Universal Circuit Tracks Pack".
The page needs to be opened from the website (or a local web server), not as a file on disk. Check your connection and reload.
"This is not a standard MIDI file".
The file isn't a .mid file, or it is damaged. Karaoke (.kar) and RIFF MIDI (.rmi) files usually work; other formats don't.
The Drums button is greyed out.
Only General MIDI drum parts (channel 10) can go on the drum tracks. Other percussion parts can go on MIDI 1 or 2.
"Everything is muted".
Click a hollow dot to unmute a part.
No sound in the preview.
Check your volume, and that no track is soloed (a dark S button). Browsers only allow sound after a click, so press Play preview again.
The song plays at the wrong speed on the Tracks.
If Tempo ÷2 or ÷4 is on, the Tracks shows the lower tempo but the song plays at its own speed. Otherwise the file may have tempo changes; the project uses the first tempo.
Nothing comes out of MIDI 1 or MIDI 2.
They send on the channels set in the Tracks' Setup view; set your other gear to match.
