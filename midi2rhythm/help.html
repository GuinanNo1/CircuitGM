MIDI2Rhythm help CircuitGM MIDI to Circuit Rhythm Converter For testing · v0.6

How MIDI2Rhythm fits a General MIDI song onto the Circuit Rhythm's eight tracks.

Quickstart

  1. Drop a MIDI file on step 1.
  2. Check step 2: every part shows its type and the track it landed on. Untick parts you don't need, to make room for ones listed as "No track left".
  3. Pick a drum kit in step 3.
  4. Press Play preview in step 4 to hear it with the RhythmGM samples.
  5. Name the project and pack, then Download pack, open it in Novation Components and send it to the Circuit Rhythm.

Eight one-note tracks

Each Circuit Rhythm step holds one note per track, but every step also has its own sample and pitch. So the Rhythm works like eight voices that play one note at a time, each able to switch instrument on any step. MIDI2Rhythm uses that in two ways: drum sounds that never hit on the same step share a track (often a whole kit fits on two or three), and parts that never play at the same time can share a track (turn this off in Advanced).

Tracks are given out in this order: drums (at most 3 tracks; 1–4 in Advanced, step 2 under "The eight tracks"), the bass, the melody, then the other parts, busiest first. The bass is the lowest line, preferring GM bass instruments. The melody is the line that is most mono, highest and busiest, preferring leads, solo instruments and voices (and parts named "lead", "melody", "vocal" and so on).

Part types

Mono
Never plays two notes at once. Goes across as it is.
Mostly mono
Only the tails of notes overlap the next note (legato playing), or overlaps are very rare. The tails are trimmed and it goes across as a mono line.
Chords
Plays several notes at once. It is turned into one line by the method in "Chords become".
Drums
The GM drum part (channel 10), played on the chosen drum kit.

Chords become…

Auto (default)
When there are free tracks once the drums, bass, melody and other parts have theirs, the part is split into its separate voices, one track each with the same sound: all of them if they fit (enough voices for 98% of its notes, up to 6), otherwise as many as the free tracks allow. Busiest chord parts are split first, and never at the cost of pushing another part off. With no room, Auto uses Smart line. Simple files such as piano pieces keep their full harmony this way. Switch it off in Advanced (step 2, under "The eight tracks").
Smart line
Picks one note at each moment by looking for the path through the whole part with the lowest total cost. It avoids big leaps (small steps sound like a real line), keeps a held note going instead of jumping off it, prefers louder, longer and higher notes, avoids doubling the song's bass note, and prefers notes near the sample's own pitch.
Top note
The highest note sounding: usually the tune in a piano part.
Bottom note
The lowest note: good for left-hand and bass parts.
Chord root
Works out each chord and plays its root, even when the chord is inverted.
Guide tones (3rd / 7th)
Plays each chord's 3rd (or 7th). These notes make a chord major, minor or dominant and usually move by a step from chord to chord, so they make a smooth line that still says what the harmony is.
Arpeggio (Advanced)
Cycles through the chord's notes at the arpeggio speed set in step 3.
Split into 2 or 3 voices, or all its voices
Each voice gets its own track with the same sound. Voices stay put: a held note keeps sounding on its track, and each new note goes to the free voice whose last note is nearest, without crossing the voices that are holding, as voice-leading would. When more notes start than there are voices, the top and bottom notes are kept and the quietest inner notes left out.

Keeping lines near the sample's note

Each RhythmInstGM sample is one recording at one pitch, named in the sample (for example "001 Grand Piano C4"). The Rhythm plays other notes by speeding the sample up or slowing it down, which changes its tone and length, so a sample played far from its own note stops sounding like the instrument. MIDI2Rhythm:

  1. moves each part by whole octaves so that as few notes as possible fall outside the range, and its middle sits near the sample's note (octave moves keep the tune and harmony intact);
  2. in Smart line, prefers chord notes near the sample's note;
  3. folds any note still outside the range an octave towards the sample's note.

The range is ±12 semitones; Advanced offers ±7, ±18 or off. The parts table (Advanced) shows each part's octave move and how many notes were folded. Unpitched instruments (Taiko, Seashore and so on) play at their own pitch.

The pack

RhythmInstGM's samples 1–112 keep their GM program numbers; slots 113–128 hold the 16 sounds of the chosen RhythmDrumGM or RhythmLatinGM kit, so GM programs 113–128 aren't available. Tinkle Bell, Agogo, Steel Drums, Woodblock, Taiko, Melodic Tom and Synth Drum play on a similar instrument; the sound effects (Reverse Cymbal to Gunshot) are left out unless you choose another sound for them in Advanced.

The arrangement works as in MIDI2Circuit: patterns of up to 32 steps, sections as scenes in one scene chain, identical patterns reused, and a new project when 8 patterns per track or 16 scenes run out. Each track can have its own step rate. Projects start from a project saved empty on the Circuit Rhythm, so mixer and FX settings are the hardware's defaults.

Still to check on a Circuit Rhythm

  • That the pack loads in Components and the projects play.
  • How far the Rhythm will pitch a sample, and how pitch and sample changes on one track's steps sound.
  • Whether a sample keeps sounding after its step's gate ends (the preview lets it ring until the track's next note).
