Simple and Advanced mode

The converter opens in simple mode: load a song, choose the parts, pick the sounds, check the arrangement and download. The settings that shape the arrangement keep defaults that suit most songs; in particular, every track keeps its notes even if the song needs more projects.

Switch on Advanced (beside the help links at the top) to see everything else. Tuning these can give better, more compact results, but may take some experimenting:

  • the base pack box (step 1);
  • octave shifts and the drum track for each Circuit pack drum part (steps 2 and 3);
  • Trim the pack, Section length, Pattern length, Tempo, Repeats, Quantise, Line up sections and Skip empty bars, the solo buttons, and the notes about what the arrangement changed (step 4);
  • project and pack colours (step 5).

Hidden settings keep their values, so switching modes never changes the pack. If you changed a setting and then switch back to simple mode, a line under the header says so. Problems that change what the pack can hold (a song longer than 64 projects, samples over the 196.6 s limit) show in both modes. The page remembers your choice in this browser.

Quickstart

  1. Load a song. Open the converter and drop a Standard MIDI file (.mid) onto step 1. You can also drop a Circuit Tracks or Circuit Rhythm pack and pick one of its projects: see converting a Circuit pack.
  2. Check the parts. The converter has already put the two busiest parts on Synth 1 and Synth 2, the next two on MIDI 1 and MIDI 2, and the drum part on Drums. Press ▶ next to a part to hear it, and click the S1, S2, M1, M2 or Drums buttons to change where it goes.
  3. Pick the sounds. Each synth gets the patch for its General MIDI instrument from the TracksGM pack; change it if you like, and pick a drum kit. (To use your own pack instead of the TracksGM pack, drop it on the Base pack box first: see using a different base pack.)
  4. Preview. Press Play preview in step 4. Click a coloured dot to mute a part, or a box to mute some bars. If the summary says the song needs several projects, see getting a song into fewer projects.
  5. Download. Press Download pack in step 5.
  6. Play it. In Novation Components, open the pack and send it to a spare pack slot on your Circuit Tracks (it replaces whatever is in that slot). Load the pack on the Tracks and open project 1. The song's sections are its scenes, in order; if there is more than one project, carry on with project 2, 3 and so on.

What you need

  • A Novation Circuit Tracks, and Novation Components (web or desktop) to send packs to it. Packs are kept on the Tracks' microSD card.
  • A Standard MIDI file, type 0 or type 1. General MIDI files work best, because the patches and drum mapping follow General MIDI. Or a Circuit Tracks or Circuit Rhythm pack saved from Components.
  • A current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). Everything happens in the browser: your file is not uploaded anywhere.

The five steps in detail

1. Load a MIDI file or Circuit pack

Base pack. The box at the top of step 1 shows the pack the converter builds on: TracksGM_final, the TracksGM pack, unless you change it. Click Use another pack…, or drop a .circuittrackspack on the box, to use a different one; Back to TracksGM_final switches back. See using a different base pack.

MIDI file. Drop a file onto the box or click it to choose one. The line under the box shows the file's tempo, time signature, length and number of parts. Each MIDI track and channel becomes a separate part. Where several parts share a name (common in type 0 files), they are named by instrument and channel instead, for example Saw Lead (ch 4).

Circuit pack. Drop a .circuittrackspack or .circuitrhythmpack on the same box to convert one of its projects, chosen in the Project list that appears. See converting a Circuit Tracks or Circuit Rhythm pack.

2. Choose the parts

Each row is one part of the song, with its General MIDI instrument, channel, note count and a picture of where it plays. The buttons on the right send it to a Circuit Tracks track:

ButtonTrackWhat it does
S1, S2Synth 1, Synth 2Play on the Tracks' own synths, with a TracksGM pack patch.
M1, M2MIDI 1, MIDI 2Send notes out over MIDI to play other gear.
DrumsDrum 1–4Spread a General MIDI drum part (channel 10) across the four drum tracks. Only drum parts can use it.

Send several parts to the same track to combine them into one: for example a brass line and a pad on Synth 2. Click a lit button again to take the part off that track. The ▶ button plays the part on its own, as it is in the file.

3. Pick the sounds

  • Synth 1 / Synth 2 patch. All 128 TracksGM pack patches are listed in General MIDI order, so the default is the patch matching the first part's instrument. Choosing one yourself stops it changing when you move parts around.
  • Drum kit. Std, Room, Power, Elec, 808, Jazz, Brush or Orch. Each kit is eight samples in the pack, and the drum tracks use that kit.
  • Octave. Each melodic track can be moved up or down one or two octaves.
  • MIDI 1 and MIDI 2 send on the MIDI channels set on the Tracks itself (in its Setup view), not on the file's channels.

4. Check the arrangement

The summary shows how many projects the song needs, how it is cut into scenes, how many bars each pattern holds, the busiest track's pattern count (8 is the most a track can hold in one project) and the project tempo.

The grid shows the song left to right, as it will play. Columns are patterns; the top rows mark where each project (P1, P2…, with its colour) and each scene starts, with the bar number. Each Circuit Tracks track has a row whose numbers are the pattern it plays; under it are the MIDI parts feeding it.

  • Mute a whole row: click its coloured dot. A hollow ring means muted. On Synth 1, Synth 2, MIDI 1 or MIDI 2 the dot mutes every part on that track; on Kick, Snare/Tom, Hi-hats or Perc it mutes just that drum sound.
  • Mute some bars: click a box. Hatched means muted; half-hatched means some of its bars are muted. Click again to unmute.
  • S solos a track in the preview only.

Mutes change the pack as well as the preview, and muting busy parts can bring the number of projects down. Mutes are kept by bar, so they stay put when you change the options below.

Play preview plays the song as the Tracks will: notes on the step grid, at most six starting on a step, one drum sound per step. Synth parts use simple stand-in sounds (not the TracksGM pack patches); drums use the kit's real samples. Changing a mute or an option while it plays carries on from the same place.

Options

OptionChoicesWhat it does
Section lengthAuto, 4, 8, 16, 32 barsHow the song is cut into scenes. Auto tries 8-bar scenes, one-pattern scenes and scenes of varying length, and keeps whichever needs the fewest projects.
Pattern lengthPer track (default), Auto, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4 stepsThe patterns' step rate, and so how many bars a pattern holds (shown in the menu): in 4/4, 2, 4 or 8 bars at the original tempo. The two automatic choices weigh notes against projects:
  • Per track: keep notes gives each track its own step rate, as on the Circuit: a track only gets longer steps (and so longer patterns) when that loses no notes, so a phrase at 1/4 steps keeps its 8-bar patterns while a busy part stays at 1/16. It keeps more notes, sometimes at the cost of more projects. The grid shows each track's rate beside its name, and a box can hold several of a track's shorter patterns (such as 1–4).
  • Auto: fewest projects gives every track the same rate and picks the fewest projects that move less than 2% of notes off their timing. Quick repeated notes that fall in one step are merged, and the notes under the grid count them.
TempoOriginal, ÷2, ÷4Lowers the project tempo so each pattern holds 2× or 4× more of the song. The song still plays at its own speed, but the Tracks shows the lower BPM, and delay, synced LFOs, arpeggios and MIDI clock run slower. Not offered below 40 BPM.
RepeatsExact, ignoring velocity, ≥90% similar, ≥75% similarWhen two stretches of a part count as the same pattern. The similar settings reuse an earlier pattern that shares that many notes, changing a few notes (reported under the grid).
QuantiseOff, 1/16, 1/8, 1/16 tripletSnaps notes to a grid before converting. Useful for played-in MIDI, so near-identical bars match.
Line up sections with repeatsOn / offLets scenes start up to 8 bars earlier when that saves a project (the song then begins after a few empty bars).
Skip empty bars at the startOn / offStarts the first scene at the first bar with notes.

5. Build the pack

  • Project name is given to every project, numbered if there are several (Song 1, Song 2…).
  • Pack name and pack colour are how the pack appears in Components.
  • Project colours: Repeating rainbow (the default) colours the projects Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Cyan, Blue, Purple, then repeats; or pick one colour from the list for all of them.

The pack holds the whole base pack (the TracksGM pack, 128 patches and 64 drum samples, unless you chose another) plus your projects in project slots 1 onwards.

Getting a song into fewer projects

Each track can hold 8 patterns in a project, and a project has 16 scenes. A song needs more projects when its parts keep changing. Try these in order, watching the projects count and the notes under the grid:

  1. Mute what you don't need. A busy part that changes every few bars (a lead line, fills) costs the most patterns.
  2. Quantise 1/16 for MIDI that was played in rather than programmed.
  3. Repeats: ≥90% or ≥75% similar. Reuses near-identical patterns; usually only a few percent of notes change.
  4. Pattern length: Auto: fewest projects. Every track on the longest steps that keep timing; merges quick repeated notes (the notes under the grid count them).
  5. Pattern length: 1/4 steps (8-bar patterns). Halves the patterns needed, but 16th notes no longer land exactly (the moved-notes count shows how many).
  6. Tempo ÷2 or ÷4. Longer patterns still, with coarser timing and slower tempo-synced effects.

Example: One More Time (150 bars) needs 4 projects with the defaults, and 2 with Quantise 1/16 and ≥75% similar, with no timing loss and about 3% of notes changed.

On the Circuit Tracks

  • Sending the pack. In Components, open the downloaded .circuittrackspack and send it to a spare pack slot; it replaces that slot's projects, patches and samples. Then load that pack on the Tracks (see Novation's Circuit Tracks user guide for the Packs view).
  • Projects. The song starts in project 1. In each project, the scenes hold the song's sections in order and are saved as a scene chain, so the project plays through its part of the song. When the song needs more than one project, move on to project 2, 3 and so on (the Tracks can queue the next project while one plays).
  • Sounds. Synth 1 and Synth 2 load the patches you chose (each project carries its own copy, so you can tweak them per project). Drum tracks use the chosen kit; Snare/Tom, Hi-hats and Perc switch between the kit's sounds step by step.
  • Editing. Everything is ordinary Circuit Tracks patterns and scenes, so you can edit, re-arrange and save as usual. Projects use the Chromatic scale so the notes play as written.

Converting a Circuit Tracks or Circuit Rhythm pack

Step 1 also takes a pack saved from Novation Components: a Circuit Tracks pack (.circuittrackspack) or a Circuit Rhythm pack (.circuitrhythmpack). The converter rebuilds one of its projects as a Circuit Tracks project, so you can re-arrange it, move it from a Circuit Rhythm to a Circuit Tracks, or put it in a smaller pack.

  • Project. Pick the project under the file details; only saved projects with notes are listed. The project is played through as the Circuit plays it (scenes in their saved order, pattern chains, step lengths, micro-steps, gates, ties and swing), and each Circuit track with notes becomes a part. Changing the project starts the parts and mutes again.
  • Parts. The parts of a Circuit Tracks project go back to their own tracks. On a Circuit Rhythm, sample tracks played at one pitch go to Drums, and tracks played at several pitches go to Synth 1 and Synth 2 (MIDI 1 and 2 would only play external gear); any sample track can be sent to Drums.
  • Drum tracks. Each part sent to Drums has a Drum 1–4 menu beside it. A Circuit Tracks drum part keeps its own drum track. On a Circuit Rhythm, each drum part gets a drum track of its own, the busiest first; a fifth or later part shares the drum track it clashes with least. By sample name places each hit by its sample's name instead (kick, snare, hats, other). A drum track plays one sound per step, so two parts sharing one can lose a few hits; the notes under the grid count them.
  • Drums. The drum tracks play the loaded pack's own samples, so step 3 shows how many it uses instead of the kit list. Each drum track plays a sample at one pitch, so pitched Circuit Rhythm steps lose their pitch on the drum tracks (the notes under the grid say so).
  • Step rates. With Pattern length Per track (the default), each track keeps its own step rate where it can, so a project that fits in one project on the Circuit usually still does.
  • Patches. A Circuit Tracks pack becomes the base pack, so Synth 1 and Synth 2 keep their own patches. A Circuit Rhythm pack has no synth patches, so the base pack (the TracksGM pack unless you change it) supplies them. You can still choose another base pack for the patches; the drums stay the loaded pack's.
  • Trim the pack (step 4, on by default) keeps only the two synth patches and the samples the song plays; unused slots are left empty, as Components stores them. Untick it to keep every patch of the base pack and every sample of the loaded pack. The line under the box shows what the pack will hold and its total sample time.
  • Circuit Rhythm samples. A Circuit Rhythm pack has 128 sample slots and a Circuit Tracks pack 64, so the samples are renumbered, the song's own first. Samples that aren't 48 kHz, 16-bit mono are converted. A Circuit Tracks pack holds 196.6 s of samples in all, and Components doesn't warn when a pack is over; the converter does.
  • Not carried over: mixer levels, effects, parameter locks and automation, and Circuit Rhythm sample settings (slice, tune, reverse) other than the steps' own pitch.

Using a different base pack

Every converted pack is built on a base pack: its synth patches and drum samples are copied into your pack unchanged, and the converted song goes into its project slots. The default is TracksGM_final, the TracksGM pack, which is still being tuned by ear; the converter always uses the latest version. You can use any other Circuit Tracks pack instead, such as your own edit of the TracksGM pack or a pack exported from Components.

  • Patches. The Synth 1 and Synth 2 lists show the pack's own patch names. The default for each synth is still the slot matching the part's General MIDI instrument (or the nearest filled slot below it), so a pack laid out in General MIDI order, like the TracksGM pack, gives the best defaults. With any other pack, choose the patches yourself.
  • Drum kits. The pack's 64 drum samples are read as eight kits of eight, each in this order: kick, snare, rim/clap, closed hat, open hat, tom, cymbal, percussion (the drum mapping picks from these). The kit list shows each group's first sample names; hover over one to see all eight. A pack laid out differently will play the wrong sounds on some drum steps.
  • Projects. The base pack's own projects are replaced by the converted song (the converter says how many there were). Its patches and samples are kept as they are.
  • Checks. The file must be a .circuittrackspack with at least one synth patch and eight sample slots; otherwise the converter says why and keeps the pack it had.
  • The base pack is only read in your browser, like the MIDI file. Reloading the page goes back to TracksGM_final.

Limits and alpha status

  • The project file format is reverse-engineered, not documented by Novation. Keep a backup of your packs.
  • Confirmed on hardware so far: packs load, synth and drum parts play, and long held notes sustain.
  • Still to confirm: the octave of notes sent on MIDI 1 and MIDI 2, and scenes playing through in order with each lasting its full length.
  • Not converted: tempo changes, time-signature changes, pitch bend, controllers (volume, pan, sustain pedal) and program changes within a part.
  • The preview's synth sounds are rough stand-ins; listen on the Tracks for the real patches.

Please report problems, with the MIDI file if you can, in the project's GitHub Issues.

Troubleshooting

The page looks out of date, or a fix hasn't appeared.
Your browser kept an old copy. Reload with Cmd + Shift + R (Mac) or Ctrl + Shift + R (Windows).
"Could not load the TracksGM pack".
The page needs to be opened from the website (or a local web server), not as a file on disk. Check your connection and reload.
"Could not use pack: …".
The file you chose as a base pack isn't a Circuit Tracks pack, or it has no patches or samples. The converter keeps using the previous pack.
"… has no saved projects with notes to convert".
Every project in the pack is empty. Save a project on the Circuit, then save the pack again from Components.
"… is a Circuit Rhythm pack, which has no synth patches, so it can't be the base pack".
A Circuit Rhythm pack dropped on the Base pack box. Drop it on the song box instead.
The drums sound wrong with my own pack.
Its samples are probably not in the kick, snare, rim/clap, closed hat, open hat, tom, cymbal, percussion order per group of eight. See using a different base pack.
"This is not a standard MIDI file".
The file isn't a .mid file, or it is damaged. Karaoke (.kar) and RIFF MIDI (.rmi) files usually work; other formats don't.
The Drums button is greyed out.
Only General MIDI drum parts (channel 10) can go on the drum tracks. Other percussion parts can go on MIDI 1 or 2.
"Everything is muted".
Click a hollow dot to unmute a part.
No sound in the preview.
Check your volume, and that no track is soloed (a dark S button). Browsers only allow sound after a click, so press Play preview again.
The song plays at the wrong speed on the Tracks.
If Tempo ÷2 or ÷4 is on, the Tracks shows the lower tempo but the song plays at its own speed. Otherwise the file may have tempo changes; the project uses the first tempo.
Nothing comes out of MIDI 1 or MIDI 2.
They send on the channels set in the Tracks' Setup view; set your other gear to match.
