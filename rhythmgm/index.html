RhythmGM General MIDI pack for the Circuit Rhythm beta

A Novation Components pack for the Circuit Rhythm that fills all 128 sample slots: the same 64 drum hits in 8 kits as TracksGM, and 64 pitched General MIDI instruments, four from each GM family. Every sample comes from GeneralUser GS.

Beta: built and checked in software, not yet loaded on a Circuit Rhythm. Keep a backup of your packs, and please report anything odd on GitHub.

Download and install

Download RhythmGM RhythmGM.circuitrhythmpack · 128 samples · 208.3 s of the 228.1 s sample limit

With Novation Components

  1. Open Novation Components (web or desktop) with your Circuit Rhythm connected.
  2. Open RhythmGM.circuitrhythmpack in Components and send it to a spare pack slot. It replaces that slot's projects, samples and Grid FX, so save anything you want to keep first.
  3. Load the pack on the Circuit Rhythm. Its project slots are empty, ready for your own projects.

Straight onto an SD card

If Components won't take the pack file, RhythmGM_SD.zip holds the same samples in the folder layout from Novation's article Manually setting up Circuit Rhythm SD card packs (Rhythm/0/PCM/00__Std Kick.wav and so on). Rename the 0 folder to a free pack number on your card, as that article describes. The Circuit Rhythm makes default Grid FX for a pack without them.

How the slots are laid out

  • Slots 1–64: drums. Eight kits of eight hits, exactly as in TracksGM, so a kit is in the same place on both packs. Each kit runs kick, snare, rim or clap, closed hat, open hat, tom, cymbal, percussion.
  • Slots 65–128: instruments. Sixty-four General MIDI instruments in GM order, four from each of the sixteen families. Each is one note, about two seconds long, with its natural attack and release.
  • Pitch. Each instrument's name ends with its root note (for example Grand Piano C4, Finger Bass C2). Play a step at that note and the sample sounds at its own pitch; other notes pitch it up or down. Basses are rooted low and bells high, so their useful range needs the least stretching.
  • Levels. Drums peak at −0.5 dBFS, as in TracksGM. Instruments are matched by loudness over their first second, never peaking above −0.5 dBFS.

How it was made

The drums are the 64 TracksGM hits, unchanged. Each instrument note was rendered from GeneralUser GS through FluidSynth (the synth it is voiced for), at velocity 100 with reverb and chorus off, summed to mono at 48 kHz and 16 bits, the format the Circuit Rhythm stores. Sustained sounds were held for two seconds and keep their release; decaying sounds ring out, up to 2.6 seconds. The script, make_rhythmgm.py, rebuilds the pack exactly; the choice of instruments and root notes is a list at its top.
